So a while back, an Anthropic scientist [met with the Pope](https://www.yahoo.com/news/science/articles/anthropic-tried-persuade-pope-ai-131853948.html) to persuade him that AI could be a concious being. Pope Leo previously in his encyclical *MAGNIFICA HUMANITAS* talked how an AI cannot be concious.

In this article, I would like to present (in my opinion) a very elegant argument as to why conciousness cannot be physical, which leads us to the conclusion that (if the said argument is correct) AI, being purely physical cannot be concious even in principle

I'm not claiming that I was the first to make this argument. There are resources at the end of the post if you want my sources.

Before we begin with the actual argument, I'd like to give a very very simplified view of the philosophical opinions on this matter.

# Mind and the Brain
Very simply put, the brain is the physical organ snuggled up in your Cranium.

The mind however is that part (either physical or non-physical) that is responsible for your subjective experience of the world. 

Now there is considerable debate on whether the mind is nothing but the brain, or if they're necessarily two different things, which we shall explore soon. 

# Materialists, Dualists and None of the Above
The very basic classification we can make is in two ways.

One school of thought says that conciousness can ultimately be reduced to something purely physical. In other words, your brain is your mind, and your mind is your brain. 

This view says that, even though we do not know how the brain works yet, we will with the advance of neuroscience map the brain entirely and then find out that all subjective experiences can be reduced down to physical, objective facts.

One might in the future, instead of saying "I'm feeling happy" might opt in to say "The part of the brain responsible for happiness is currently being stimulated by this particular chemical which causes me to subjectively feel happy" (try writing romance novels in that language :p)

The other view is the negation of the previous, that conciousness isn't completely material, that your mind is not equal to your brain. The most famous proponent of these are dualists (coming mostly from the works of Decartes), but other non-materialist schools also exist (such as Thomism).

# Intentionality
We will have to look at a small concept before we go ahead. The concept is what philosophers of the mind call "intentionality".

It may be understood as that property by which something "points-to" or "is directed towards" something other than itself.

We know intentionality is present, because we ourselves experience it firsthand. We can look at things and think of them as "pointing-towards" or referring to an abstract concept. Denying this has some self-contradictory implications, which we will also see.

 You can say that, a (badly) drawn picture of a circle "points-to" or "is directed towards" the idea of a ball, or an apple, or an asteroid (whatever it is). That is, it represents a ball, or an apple or whatever circlular thing you want it to be.
 

So what's the big deal about intentionality? Well, it poses a very big difficulty for the materialist position.

# The good stuff

Consider this picture:

What does this represent?

Now some might say it represents a ball, others an apple, maybe an asteroid or a planet perhaps? Could be any of these things.  
But ultimately you may realize that none of the physical facts (in this case, the pixels that light up on your screen) tell you what this thing represent. It's only when you ask the creator of the image (which is me btw :D ) do you know what it represents.

Now someone might say "write what it represents below the image":


![A picture with a badly drawn circle with text below it "this is a Ball!"](../static/images/posts/ai_concious/subtitledwhatisthis.png)

We can now know that the image does represent a ball, but that's only because we read the text below it. But this text is purely physical too, therefore physical things can be intentional!

Well no, we've just moved the problem further. To a person who doesn't read English, those are just meaningless scribbles, which themselves require intentionality to understand. Moreover, to the person who can read English, he or she is just applying intentionality which is present in his or her mind to the text.

He or she is simply interpreting the text to be pointing towards the meaning that "the above image represents a ball", but the act of interpreting itself is an act that uses intentionality, which is found in the mind. And the mind being the thing in question (that whether it is physical or not) cannot be used to defend the claim that intentionality is possible in physical objects without begging the question. 

So all in all, it doesn't seem that physical things can be intentional.

But if physical things cannot be intentional, and we do have intentionality, then that means that the mind isn't physical and the brain is not equal to the mind! Materialism is found to be false. 

Now a big part of being concious, is having intentionality. When intentionality is absent, conciousness also goes away. So, our AI models being purely physical (where the physical parts in question are electrons in logical gates and electronic devices, combining to make up a program which is the AI), cannot be concious.

# Possible objections
Here are some possible objections that someone might reply with:

**Objection 1: It would seem that purely physical things can be intentional. We have machines such as a calculator, which can point to something else (namely the numbers/digits on its screen)**

I answer that, the case of the calculator is much like the case of the image of the circle with the text written under it. For a person who isn't familiar with the Hindu-Arabic numeric system, it is a meaningless arrangement of lines and points towards nothing. But for those who are used to it, they are just using intentionality to understand the lines, but then we'd be begging the question.

**Objection 2: It would seem that purely physical things can be concious, because I reject the claim intentionality is real**

This ones special because apart from going against common sense and your own experience, it undermines itself by being self-contradictory. 

Suppose intentionality isn't actually real. Then nothing can "point to" or be about something else. This also means that your own thoughts aren't about something else. You aren't really reading this line right now. You aren't even thinking about this line now, because thinking would imply intentionality, which supposedly doesn't exist.

But all rational thought is intentional. Your argument uses/references logical rules such as "modus ponens". But since intentionality isn't real, then your argument isn't really referencing "modus ponens" or any other logical tool for that matter, and hence cannot be accepted as being correct (due to it not being an argument from logic/rationality).

# Conclusion
The Pope's position I think, has been vindicated. If however, we could somehow add the non-physical aspect into the machine, we might be able to get a concious AI. But as it stands now, the type of AI that we have cannot be concious
